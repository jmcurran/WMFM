The purpose of this study is to model the final exam mark based on whether students attend regularly (No, Yes), and how well they did in the test (mark out of 20). The researcher wants to predict how well they will do, which is a different question to how well would someone do on average.

The effect of attendance of attending regularly is approximately 8 marks (95% confidence interval: 3.8 to 12.2). Each extra mark in the test  is worth approximately 3.5 marks (95% c.i.: 3--4) in the final exam. This indicates that regular class attendance and doing well in the test have a positive effect on average on the final exam mark.

This model explains approximately 62% of the variation in exam marks, suggesting that whilst attendance and test mark are important factors, there are factors which may explain the variation.

If this student attend class regularly and scores 15/20 in the test then the predicted exam mark is 67.50 (95% prediction interval 44.6 to 90.5). This prediction interval is fairly wide, ranging from a fail (D) to an strong pass (A+), so there is no guarantee that the student will do well in the final exam based on this information.
